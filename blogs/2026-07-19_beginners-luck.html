Beginner's Luck:
Notes from a Crochet Workshop in Eindhoven

Date
July 19, 2026

Read
4 min

Author
Farah Tlemcani

Not everything this year has been about job hunting, even though it's felt that way lately. This weekend I spent four straight hours teaching a room full of strangers how to crochet a bag, at Al Warsha Studio in Eindhoven, and I think it did more for my sense of actually belonging here than most of what I've written about on this blog so far.

I've been leaning on this hobby more than I expected to since moving here. It's how I've made new friends, earned a little money on the side, and kept doing the one thing I love most that has absolutely nothing to do with dashboards or requirements documents. Crochet doesn't care what your CV says.

The Design

I designed the bag before anything else, on purpose. Two simple stitches, nothing fancy, the actual basics. The goal was that everyone walks out four hours later holding something real, a full, stylish, wearable bag, not just "I learned how to chain 80 and make a foundation row." Concrete beats impressive, especially on day one.

The room itself was the best part before we'd even picked up a hook. Dutch, Indian, Syrian, Egyptian, Palestinian, all in one room, all leaning over the same two stitches at the same time.

The Accidental Lesson

I told everyone upfront: don't follow my pattern blindly. If you mess up a stitch, that's not a mistake to panic over, that might just be a new design decision. Keep going and see what it turns into.

They took that far more seriously than I expected. By the end we had a full spread of colors and shapes and little personal touches nobody else in the room had, and if I'm honest, a lot of them made mine look boring by comparison.

That actually taught me something I didn't walk in expecting to learn. Being a beginner is a real advantage. Once you know the rules, once a process is fully internalized, you follow it automatically, and what you make is structured and clean and beautiful, and also reproducible, the same shape everyone who knows the same rules would make. Beginners don't have that constraint yet. They experiment because they don't know they're not supposed to, and what comes out the other side is stranger and more personal and, more often than not, better.
